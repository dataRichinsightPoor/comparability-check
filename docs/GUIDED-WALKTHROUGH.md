# Raji versus engineered cells: a guided comparison

Data-Rich, Insight-Poor CCLX · 99 Small Problems · No. 07

Comparability Check v0.6.0. All numbers are synthetic and generated by the shared model. Raji is held stable by assumption, and no observed genomic cause or antibody defect is inferred. The final reporter is a generic conceptual extension, not rituximab biology.

Each paired step declares what changes and what stays fixed. Steps are controlled thought experiments, not an uninterrupted longitudinal dataset; later steps may reset settings explicitly.

## Establish the reference comparison

Changed: No property differs yet. Both modeled populations begin with 100% positive cells and 100,000 accessible sites per positive cell.

Fixed: The same one-site antibody K = 1 nM, cell count, volume, detection summary and equilibrium assumptions apply.

Question: If these curves overlap, have we proved that the real cell lines are interchangeable?

Answer: No. The curves overlap because their relevant properties were set equal in this synthetic model. Agreement under stipulated conditions is not experimental evidence of equivalence.

## Lose positive cells, not binding strength

Changed: The engineered positive fraction falls from 1 to 0.5 over 20 modeled passages. Sites per remaining positive cell stay at 100,000.

Fixed: Antibody K, per-positive-cell site density, cell count and negligible-depletion approximation remain fixed.

Question: Must the normalized binding EC50 increase when half the cells lose expression?

Answer: No. In this model, the all-cell signal plateau halves while the binding EC50 remains 1 nM. The remaining positive cells retain the same binding curve.

| State | Cell context | Positive fraction | Sites / positive cell | Plateau / Raji | Binding EC50, nM | Tracer IC50, nM | Generic reporter IC50, nM |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Before | Raji reference | 1 | 100000 | 1 | 1 | not interpreted | not interpreted |
| Before | Engineered population | 1 | 100000 | 1 | 1 | not interpreted | not interpreted |
| After | Raji reference | 1 | 100000 | 1 | 1 | not interpreted | not interpreted |
| After | Engineered population | 0.5 | 100000 | 0.5 | 1 | not interpreted | not interpreted |

What this establishes in the model: Loss of positive cells can reduce population-mean binding signal without changing the antibody binding parameter or the normalized midpoint.

What it does not establish: That a measured signal loss came from poor integration, rather than silencing, selection, altered accessibility or another change.

Next measurement: Measure positive fraction and the distribution within positive cells separately; retain absolute signal and a consistent gating definition.

Smax ∝ pρ. Reducing p from 1 to 0.5 halves the all-cell arithmetic-mean plateau.

## Now reduce expression within positive cells

Changed: At the same passage and positive fraction, the modeled density halves from 100,000 to 50,000 accessible sites per positive cell.

Fixed: Positive fraction stays at 0.5; antibody K, cell count, volume and all-cell signal summary remain unchanged.

Question: What happens to the plateau when both fraction and density are half their starting values?

Answer: It becomes one quarter of the Raji reference plateau: 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25. The normalized binding EC50 is still 1 nM under the negligible-depletion assumption.

| State | Cell context | Positive fraction | Sites / positive cell | Plateau / Raji | Binding EC50, nM | Tracer IC50, nM | Generic reporter IC50, nM |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Before | Raji reference | 1 | 100000 | 1 | 1 | not interpreted | not interpreted |
| Before | Engineered population | 0.5 | 100000 | 0.5 | 1 | not interpreted | not interpreted |
| After | Raji reference | 1 | 100000 | 1 | 1 | not interpreted | not interpreted |
| After | Engineered population | 0.5 | 50000 | 0.25 | 1 | not interpreted | not interpreted |

What this establishes in the model: Positive fraction and density contribute separately to the same population-mean signal. Their effects multiply in this deliberately simple signal model.

What it does not establish: Which process changed from a single mean-fluorescence value, or which genomic mechanism produced the phenotype.

Next measurement: Track the full expression distribution across passage and use independent DNA/transcript evidence if integration or silencing is the claim.

Smax / Raji = p × (ρ / ρRaji) = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25.

## Normalize the curves and lose the amplitude information

Changed: Only the displayed response changes: absolute relative signal becomes the fraction of each curve's own saturation signal.

Fixed: Every biological and assay parameter is identical before and after. This is a change of representation, not a rescue of the engineered cells.

These panels show different y-axis quantities. Their matching numerical scale does not make absolute signal and fractional occupancy the same measurement.

Question: Does the overlap after normalization mean the engineered population has recovered?

Answer: No. The underlying engineered plateau is still 0.25 of the reference. Normalization divides away that amplitude difference, leaving overlapping occupancy curves.

What this establishes in the model: Normalized binding curves can agree while their absolute signal and cell-expression distributions differ substantially.

What it does not establish: Equivalent cell substrates, adequate assay dynamic range, or biological recovery.

Next measurement: Keep the absolute plateaus, background, positive fraction and signal-to-noise evidence beside the normalized curves.

## Change the gate, not the cells

Changed: The arithmetic mean is now calculated only among positive cells instead of across all cells.

Fixed: The engineered population remains 50% positive with 50,000 sites per positive cell. Ligand consumption still depends on the full sample.

Question: If the plateau rises from 0.25 to 0.5 after gating, has target expression increased?

Answer: No. The summary population changed. Positive-only averaging removes the factor for the 50% negative cells but retains the lower per-positive-cell density.

| State | Cell context | Positive fraction | Sites / positive cell | Plateau / Raji | Binding EC50, nM | Tracer IC50, nM | Generic reporter IC50, nM |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Before | Raji reference | 1 | 100000 | 1 | 1 | not interpreted | not interpreted |
| Before | Engineered population | 0.5 | 50000 | 0.25 | 1 | not interpreted | not interpreted |
| After | Raji reference | 1 | 100000 | 1 | 1 | not interpreted | not interpreted |
| After | Engineered population | 0.5 | 50000 | 0.5 | 1 | not interpreted | not interpreted |

What this establishes in the model: A gating change can increase reported signal without improving the cells or the antibody.

What it does not establish: A biological increase in expression, a change in affinity, or a result about median fluorescence or threshold-based gates.

Next measurement: Report the gate, its population fraction and the within-gate distribution. Compare like summaries before attributing changes to biology.

All-cell plateau ∝ pρ; positive-only plateau ∝ ρ. This model uses arithmetic means, not medians.

## Account for the antibody consumed by the cells

Changed: At a deliberately higher load of 1,000,000 cells per 100 µL, replace the negligible-depletion approximation with explicit ligand mass balance.

Fixed: Both panels use the same high cell load, antibody K and cell states. The before panel is an approximation check, not a second experiment.

The left panel intentionally uses a strained approximation. Interpret the right panel as the same stipulated system with conservation enforced, not as observed experimental truth.

Question: Can lower target abundance produce a lower total-dose binding EC50 with the same antibody?

Answer: Yes, in this one-site equilibrium model. Fewer sites consume less ligand, so half occupancy requires less total added antibody. The lower midpoint does not show stronger intrinsic binding.

| State | Cell context | Positive fraction | Sites / positive cell | Plateau / Raji | Binding EC50, nM | Tracer IC50, nM | Generic reporter IC50, nM |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Before | Raji reference | 1 | 100000 | 1 | 1 | not interpreted | not interpreted |
| Before | Engineered population | 0.5 | 50000 | 0.25 | 1 | not interpreted | not interpreted |
| After | Raji reference | 1 | 100000 | 1 | 1.83027 | not interpreted | not interpreted |
| After | Engineered population | 0.5 | 50000 | 0.25 | 1.20757 | not interpreted | not interpreted |

What this establishes in the model: Finite ligand supply can create a cell-context EC50 difference while K stays fixed. The engineered population has less target and therefore a smaller depletion shift.

What it does not establish: That depletion explains a particular real experiment, or that lower expression always shifts an intact antibody's apparent EC50 left.

Next measurement: Check cell number, accessible target load and volume; test equilibrium and depletion assumptions while retaining absolute signal.

EC50,total = K + RT/2. The finite-supply solver conserves both ligand and receptor.

## Change the tracer, not the antibody

Changed: The homologous tracer concentration increases from 1 to 10 nM in an ideal competitive-displacement assay.

Fixed: Antibody K, cells, passage and negligible-depletion approximation remain fixed. Labeling is assumed not to change the tracer's binding.

Tracer displacement is a binding assay. It is not a measurement of downstream CD20 function.

Question: Does the higher competition IC50 mean the antibody binds more weakly?

Answer: No. The model's competition IC50 rises from 2 to 11 nM because IC50 = K + tracer, while the direct-binding EC50 remains 1 nM.

| State | Cell context | Positive fraction | Sites / positive cell | Plateau / Raji | Binding EC50, nM | Tracer IC50, nM | Generic reporter IC50, nM |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Before | Raji reference | 1 | 100000 | 1 | 1 | 2 | not interpreted |
| Before | Engineered population | 0.5 | 50000 | 0.25 | 1 | 2 | not interpreted |
| After | Raji reference | 1 | 100000 | 1 | 1 | 11 | not interpreted |
| After | Engineered population | 0.5 | 50000 | 0.25 | 1 | 11 | not interpreted |

What this establishes in the model: Binding and competitive-displacement midpoints depend on different assay conditions, even for the same antibody.

What it does not establish: Loss of antibody quality, downstream functional blockade, or a universal conversion between binding EC50 and blocking IC50.

Next measurement: Specify the tracer, its concentration and binding behavior, and check depletion and equilibrium before comparing competition endpoints.

For the ideal homologous, nondepleting case: binding EC50 = K; competition IC50 = K + T.

## Ask what converts occupancy into function

Changed: The output changes from tracer displacement to an explicitly hypothetical reporter driven by occupied tracer sites, with a half-response threshold of 1,000 occupied sites.

Fixed: Antibody K, tracer, cell states and occupancy are unchanged. Only an additional occupancy-to-response mapping is introduced.

Conceptual extension only. The reporter is NOT CD20 signaling, CDC, ADCC or a validated rituximab functional-blocking assay.

Question: Must a functional IC50 equal the binding or displacement midpoint?

Answer: No. In this hypothetical h = 1 reporter, response reserve gives a 102 nM IC50 for the reference and 52 nM for the lower-density engineered cells, while both direct-binding EC50s remain 1 nM. These are model outputs, not rituximab functional measurements.

| State | Cell context | Positive fraction | Sites / positive cell | Plateau / Raji | Binding EC50, nM | Tracer IC50, nM | Generic reporter IC50, nM |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Before | Raji reference | 1 | 100000 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 102 |
| Before | Engineered population | 0.5 | 50000 | 0.25 | 1 | 2 | 52 |
| After | Raji reference | 1 | 100000 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 102 |
| After | Engineered population | 0.5 | 50000 | 0.25 | 1 | 2 | 52 |

What this establishes in the model: An assumed response function can separate functional and occupancy midpoints. Lower density can reduce response reserve rather than improve the antibody.

What it does not establish: Rituximab's CD20 signaling, CDC, ADCC, therapeutic efficacy, or the correct response law for a real blocking assay.

Next measurement: Define what is blocked and measure baseline function, maximal inhibition, stimulus dependence and timing. Test a biological occupancy-to-response relationship rather than borrowing this toy function.

For h = 1: IC50,reporter = (K + T) × (1 + x(0)/S50). This follows from the chosen transfer function, not from a CD20 mechanism.

## Scientific boundaries

Expression loss can arise in integrated transgenes and does not by itself diagnose integration failure ([Cabrera et al., 2022](https://www.cell.com/cell-systems/fulltext/S2405-4712(22)00464-1)). The one-site depletion and competition equations require their stated equilibrium and concentration assumptions ([Hulme and Trevethick, 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3000649/)). Fluorescent rituximab displacement on Raji cells has experimental precedent as competition binding, separate from downstream activity ([Visser et al., 2013](https://d-nb.info/1196862753/34)). The generic reporter used here is a stipulated mathematical transfer function, not an empirical finding about rituximab.
